2025 Annual Water Quality Report

Every household served by Harbor Bay receives this report once a year. It sets out what we tested for, what we found, and what we did about the one result that exceeded a federal action level.

Published 14 March 2026 · Covers 1 January to 31 December 2025 · Prepared under 40 CFR 141.153 · Contact: quality@harborbaywater.example

Harbor Bay draws its water from the Calder Reservoir and from four groundwater wells in the Eastfield aquifer. Over 2025 we collected 3,184 samples and tested them for 91 separate contaminants. Almost everything we measured sat well inside federal and state limits. One did not, and most of this report is about that one.

What we found

Lead concentrations in the Eastfield service area rose through the spring of 2025 and crossed the federal action level of 15 parts per billion in July, reaching 16.2 ppb. Lead does not occur in the reservoir or in the wells. It enters the water from lead service lines and older household plumbing, and how much of it dissolves depends on how corrosive the water is when it reaches those pipes.

Lead, monthly average

parts per billion · Eastfield service area · 2025

In August we increased the orthophosphate dose at the Eastfield treatment plant. Orthophosphate builds a thin protective film on the inside of pipework, which slows the rate at which lead dissolves into the water passing through it. Concentrations fell through the autumn and were back to 3.2 ppb by December.

How your water is treated

Raw water passes through seven stages before it reaches the distribution mains. Corrosion control, the stage adjusted in August, sits between disinfection and storage.

Regulatory compliance

The two paragraphs that follow are reproduced from the notice we are required to publish. They are written to satisfy the regulation rather than to be read.

Pursuant to the monitoring and reporting obligations imposed under 40 CFR Part 141 Subpart I, the supplier of water is required to determine compliance with the lead action level by reference to the ninetieth percentile of all tap samples collected during each monitoring period, such that an exceedance is deemed to have occurred if, and only if, the concentration of lead in more than ten percent of the samples so collected is greater than 0.015 milligrams per litre, notwithstanding that individual results in excess of that figure may be recorded without an exceedance being triggered, and provided further that any system which exceeds the action level shall thereafter be subject to the public education requirements of 141.85 and the corrosion control treatment requirements of 141.81 until such time as the system demonstrates compliance for two consecutive monitoring periods.

Where a system serving a population of more than fifty thousand persons has failed to meet the optimal water quality parameters designated by the primacy agency for two consecutive six-month periods, the system shall, within twelve months of the end of the second such period, submit a recommendation for source water treatment or additional corrosion control treatment, whereupon the primacy agency shall make a determination as to whether such treatment is required, and the system shall complete installation of any treatment so required within twenty-four months of that determination, subject to any extension the primacy agency may grant in writing upon a showing that completion within the said period is not practicable.

What this means for you

If your home was built before 1988 it may have a lead service line. Running the cold tap for thirty seconds before drinking clears water that has been standing in the pipe overnight. We will test your tap free of charge; the waiting list is currently about three weeks. Households on the Eastfield network are being prioritised.

Words used in this report

ppb — parts per billion. One ppb is roughly one drop in an Olympic pool.

Action level — the concentration at which a utility must take corrective steps. For lead it is 15 ppb. It is a trigger for action, not a safety threshold.

Ninetieth percentile — compliance is judged on this, not on the worst single sample. One high reading is not automatically a violation.

Corrosion control — treatment that stops pipe metal dissolving into the water.

Primacy agency — the state body that enforces federal drinking water rules.
